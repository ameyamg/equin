# call-handoff

An op an agent runs in a Bash call — `build`, `push`, `check`, `test`, in the
foreground or the background — made unable to die at the call's time limit,
whenever and however that limit comes, a sleeping Mac included.

## Why

The harness ends a call at its `timeout`: 600 s in the foreground, at most 2 h
in the background. It does so by killing the call's whole process tree, and
the limit runs on the **wall clock** — a Mac that sleeps through it fires it
the moment it wakes. An op that is a descendant of the call dies with it,
however little of its time was awake:

- On 2026-11-22 (on battery, the Mac asleep most of every hour) four agent
  builds were SIGTERMed at 31, 61, 121 and 121 min of wall clock after 0.9,
  0.5, 2.4 and 1.0 min awake (op log: `build-1795314400-qbng8n`,
  `-1795322586-osbieh`, `-1795343567-5hlm5g`, `-1795344567-lbj1mk`); a build
  needs about 10 min awake. Their agents read "stopped at the 2 h limit, do not
  restart" and parked the work.
- A foreground `check` / `test` is killed at 600 s with `Command timed out` and
  nothing else: no task id, no verdict.

A `setsid` / own-process-group child does not escape: measured 2026-11-24, the
harness's kill reached a `setsid` grandchild of a background call; a child
re-parented to launchd (its parent gone) survived.

## How

1. The `call-deadline` guard (tooling/guards) sees a call running a hand-off op
   (`HANDOFF_OPS`), raises a foreground call's timeout to 600 s (a background
   one is at 2 h already, `background-timeout`) and exports
   `EQUIN_CALL_DEADLINE` = now + timeout − `HANDOFF_MARGIN_MS` (60 s).
2. The CLI mapper (`cli/bin/register-commands.ts`) sees the deadline on a
   top-level hand-off command and calls `runHandedOff` instead of the action.
3. `runHandedOff` re-runs the same argv **off the call**: through
   `cli/internal/op-wrapper.ts`, whose `detach` role (a session leader) starts
   the `wrap` role and exits, so `wrap` and the op under it are re-parented to
   launchd. `wrap` writes the op's `{ code, signal }` to a status file when it
   ends, however it ends. Output goes to a temp file the launcher streams to
   its own stdout; the deadline is unset for the op, so nothing below it hands
   off again. `OP_HANDED_OFF` names the op's owner — the agent's Claude Code
   process (`CLAUDE_PID`) — and its orphan guard follows that pid.
   - The op finishes in time → its exit code is the call's (from the status
     file). Indistinguishable from running in place.
   - The deadline comes first → print the hand-off (`./equin await <op>`, the
     output file), exit **70** (`await`'s "still running"), leave the op
     running.
   - A signal reaches the launcher **at or past the deadline** → that is the
     harness's own limit (after a sleep it fires on wake, possibly before the
     launcher's timer): same hand-off. A SIGKILL that leaves the launcher no
     handler costs only the notice — the op is out of the call's reach, and
     `wrap` judges a launcher gone past the deadline the same way.
   - A signal **before the deadline** (Esc, TaskStop) → SIGTERM the op's
     group, as an in-place op would have died: a hand-off is the limit's
     answer, not an interrupt's (`isCallLimitSignal`, the one judgement).
     The launcher's handlers do it for a catchable signal; `wrap` watches the
     launcher's pid (every 2 s) and does it for every other way the launcher
     can die before the deadline — a SIGKILL, a crash.
   - The reader of the launcher's stdout goes away (`| head`) → streaming
     stops, the op does not: its output stays in the file.
   - The wrapper vanishes with no status (killed on its own) → the rest of
     the group is stopped, said so, exit 1; a 15 s heartbeat finds it, since
     the wrapper is no child to wait on.
4. A handed-off op ends (exit 140, its group with it, its grant released) once
   its owning agent is gone: `./equin await` is the agent's only handle on it,
   so nobody could ever read its verdict. Before that rule, a `./equin test`
   handed off under a `| grep | head` spun for 11 h on a cpu slot after its
   conversation had ended (2026-11-23).

The launcher is not an op: it writes no marker and takes no grant. The op is
the op, so `./equin await` finds it by its marker like any other.

`HANDOFF_OPS` (`core`) is the one list both the guard and the mapper read;
`FOREGROUND_OPS` is the subset `background-ops` lets run in the foreground
(`check`, `test` — `build` / `push` would hand off on most runs).

A checkout runs its own guards with its own CLI (main's guards judge only
while a checkout's own fail to load), so the env name could change with no
transition: a checkout not yet rebased uses the old name on both sides.
