# plugin-system

The plugin system page at `/about/plugin-system` — how equin is put together,
as one scroll-driven isometric zoom out over a city of glass bricks. Ported
from the prototype `proto-1794379870-rcqzur`, which stays the design reference,
at the options picked on it: `block: #63a5f8`, `swap: #fb923c`,
`pick: #fb923c`, `glow: soft`. Re-check the picks (`./equin prototype options
<id>`) before porting a change; only those picks are implemented.

- The heading (`WebsiteHero`, "Everything is a plugin") sits in the flow; the
  picture starts just under it and its top edge melts into the page (a mask on
  the stage), so the heading's glow runs on into it.
- A pinned stage (one screen of the pane's viewport) draws the picture; eight
  chapter cards scroll over it (`internal/chapters.ts`, the prototype's copy
  verbatim). The picture has its own timeline p. A chapter stands at one place
  on it and holds there while its card is read, or spans a stretch of it: then
  its card is sticky and stays while the scroll plays the stretch (Customize: a
  feature removed, then the database swapped; Standalone apps: three builds lit
  one after the other).
- Where every brick is, is a pure function of p. Only the light plays on its
  own: bricks breathing, a band climbing the towers, the pointer's highlight
  (one plugin up close, a sub-system around the first app, a whole app once the
  city stands) and a click's ring.
- The chapter strip (segments filling as the page passes each chapter, the
  chapter's name) is pinned under the site header while the zoom is read; a
  segment jumps to its chapter. The header frosts once content scrolls under it
  (`WebsiteChrome headerGlass`).

## How it is built

React renders the markup ONCE (`components/plugin-system-zoom.tsx`: the hero,
then the memoised zoom with no props) and hands the DOM to imperative owners,
exactly as the prototype's script owned its page. Never let React re-render it.

- `internal/scroll.ts` — the scroll skeleton: the page's measures (`--screen`,
  `--ps-head`), scroll → p, the cards' lit state, the chapter strip. Everything
  scrolls inside the PANE: reads go through the pane's scroll container
  (`findScrollParent`), never `window`.
- `internal/world.ts` — the city, generated once from a fixed seed: the
  terraced ground, the towers (a few shapes, Mail as the first app, the Agent
  manager in the middle), the sub-systems of the foundation, the standalone
  builds. The ORDER of its random draws is part of the design: every brick's
  size and arrival comes from it.
- `internal/scene.ts` — the picture at p: the camera keys, every brick's place
  and colour per frame, the labels (HTML over the canvas), the pointer and the
  click. ONE rAF loop, only while the stage is on screen and the tab visible;
  p eases after the scroll's target.
- `internal/renderer.ts` — the WebGL side (three.js): one instanced shader for
  the bricks and the studs (faces, front edges, hidden edges), a floor grid and
  a soft bloom. three.js is this plugin's own dependency and loads only with
  the page's chunk.

Everything registered (the scroll listener, the pointer and click listeners,
the in-view watcher, the visibility listener, the rAF loop, the section-reach
observers) is disposed on unmount, and the GL context is handed back.

## The palette

The picture is light added to a black ground, the same in either mode, so:

- the scene's colours are declared once on `.plugin-system`
  (`components/plugin-system-zoom.css`): the CSS reads them for the labels, the
  scene reads the four it draws with from the root at mount;
- the page wears `pluginSystemNightTheme` (`internal/theme.ts`): the site
  theme's dark values pinned in both modes, for the heading, the chapter text
  and the site header over it.

This is the third such picture on the site (after the iceberg and the vision
zoom); it is not a precedent for a component's colour.

Analytics: each chapter card records `section_reach` as `plugin-system/<key>`
(`plugin`, `slots`, `infrastructure`, `app`, `customize`, `system`,
`standalone`, `personal-os`); the heading records `plugin-system/hero`.
