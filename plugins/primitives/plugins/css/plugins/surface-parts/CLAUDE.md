# surface-parts

**Experimental: no consumer yet, do not adopt.** The working exhibit of
[`research/2026-11-27-global-surface-parts.md`](../../../../../../research/2026-11-27-global-surface-parts.md),
which decides whether this becomes the way surfaces are built.

A region of a surface is a grid with three named column lines (`web/internal/lines.css`):
`edge` (the box edge), `fill` (where a row's hover fill starts), `text` (where
text and ordinary content start). A part sits on a line; unknown content lands
on `text` by doing nothing. No part pads inline and none has a negative margin.
A grouping part (`Panel.Section`) is a relay — a subgrid handing the same lines
to its children — so nesting adds no inset.

`Panel` is one family built this way (a searchable menu). Its parts take no
`className` and no padding prop; `data-geo` is the one attribute they forward,
for the layout-geometry harness.

Exhibits: `surface-parts/menu` (everything that is text starts on one line,
across three regions and two depths) and `surface-parts/traps` / `traps-pad`
(the rail doc's three traps, attempted with parts and measured).

**Two mechanisms, one set of parts.** Under `[data-part-mechanism="pad"]` the
same parts are laid out the other way: the region pads to `text` and a part on
`fill` / `edge` steps out by the region's own distance; a box that pads or
scrolls without being a region must reset it (`part-reset`). The `wrappers` /
`wrappers-pad` exhibits hold both to the same assertions, and their
`KNOWN_MISSES` are the measured comparison: lines break behind a
`display: contents` wrapper and behind any plain wrapper box (a row's label
lands mid-row); pad breaks only under a padder that does not reset. The doc
recommends pad.

Geometry assertions are about x only: check a screenshot too
(`exhibit-shot.ts --id surface-parts/menu`).
